# EXPERIMENT 001 — THE SOUND CARRIER
## ABC notation as the pitch bank, Braille as the second bank: a mapping test for Japanese

**Ordered by Johnathan "Qasparr" Monroe, 2026-10-28:** "If we use ABC
notation to map Japanese with Braille as the 2nd Bank fallback. Does that
help Map Japanese? Since Japanese uses pitch and tone in flow as a form
change in meaning. This should be able to be mapped with both of these,
using sound as the carrier… Worth the experiment to find out."

**Standing law of this research (his ruling, 2026-10-09):** a research
attempt to attest a bridge — demonstrated or not. No bridge is decreed.
Results below are tiered: ATTESTED (independent sources), DEMONSTRATED
(constructed and checked here), HYPOTHESIS (not yet tested).

## I. The test

Japanese (Tokyo) is a **pitch-accent** language: a word's high/low pattern
over its morae can be the only difference between meanings (ATTESTED,
standard linguistics). If sound is the carrier, a notation that carries
pitch exactly should separate those meanings, and a second bank that
carries the syllable skeleton should back it.

**The minimal triplet はし (hashi)** — three words, one segmental string
(ATTESTED accent patterns; が = the following particle, where the patterns
finish):

| Word | Meaning | Accent | Pattern (with が) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 端 | edge | heiban (0) | L H H |
| 橋 | bridge | odaka (2) | L H L |
| 箸 | chopsticks | atamadaka (1) | H L L |

## II. Bank 1 — ABC notation (the pitch bank). DEMONSTRATED.

Scheme (constructed, documented so it can be checked): one mora = one
quarter note; H mora = degree 5 (G); L mora = degree 2 (D); the particle
が appended, because two of the three patterns differ only in the tail.

- 端 (edge): `D G G`
- 橋 (bridge): `D G D`
- 箸 (chopsticks): `G D D`

**Result: the three encodings are pairwise distinct — the notation
separates the full minimal triplet.** Where pitch carries meaning, ABC
carries pitch. The bridge holds at the phonological level.

## III. Bank 2 — Braille (the fallback bank). ATTESTED system; pitch boundary found.

Japanese has a native Braille (tenji), standardized and kana-based
(ATTESTED): the vowel (dan) patterns ⠁ ⠃ ⠉ ⠋ ⠊ take consonant-row dots —
は行 adds dots 3&6, さ行 adds dots 5&6 — so は = ⠥, し = ⠳, and
はし = **⠥⠳**.

**Zero point, named:** tenji carries the syllable skeleton exactly and the
pitch **not at all** — all three はし words are the same two cells. The
banks are therefore complementary, not redundant: **ABC is the pitch bank;
Braille is the segment bank; the fallback preserves everything except the
one feature the primary bank exists to carry.** A fallback that must not
lose pitch has to ride an annotation (accent class number) alongside the
cells — an addition, and labeled as one if ever built.

## IV. The boundary of the bridge (zero points)

1. **Sound maps phonology, not kanji semantics.** In speech, pitch
separates 橋/箸/端; in writing, kanji does. A sound-carrier bridge
joins the spoken language; the written semantic layer needs its own
bank (kantenji — 8-dot Braille kanji — exists, ATTESTED, not tested here).
2. **Japanese pitch accent is pattern-level, not lexical tone.** The
contrast load is small (three classes over the word), which is why the
demonstration is clean — and why it must not be over-read.
3. **Extension rule (the author's "if it works, do the same for the Asian
languages"):** the method transfers exactly where pitch or tone is
phonemic. Mandarin: four contour tones + neutral — ABC draws contours
natively, a STRONGER fit (demonstration: mā level = one held high note;
má rising = D→G; mǎ fall-rise = G→D→G; mà falling = G→D — constructed,
same scheme family). Thai (5 tones) and Vietnamese (6): same class.
Korean (standard Seoul): no phonemic tone — **the carrier finds nothing
to carry; the method's boundary is there**, and the honest report is
that the bridge does not extend to it by this road.

## V. The other two proposed bridges — weighed, not decreed

**Sanskrit.** His proposal: the alphabet recognized as Kama Sutra
positions, via vector-scalar matrices and mirroring. The desk's finding:
an attested bridge of that *shape* already exists — the varṇamālā is
ordered by **articulation position**, velar → palatal → retroflex →
dental → labial: the alphabet is a map of the vocal body's positions, a
form-flow system by construction (ATTESTED). The glyph-resemblance claim
( letterforms as body positions) is HYPOTHESIS. A fair test exists and is
named **Experiment 002** (not run; no result claimed): skeletonize glyphs
to vectors, mirror-normalize, nearest-neighbour across scripts against a
shuffled null; a bridge is attested only if the true pairing beats the
null by a stated margin.

**Arabic.** His proposal: the text "looks like 🔥 visually." The attested
structure beneath the intuition: Arabic letters change form by position
in the flow — isolated / initial / medial / final — a genuine
form-change-in-flow system, and the script runs right-to-left, so
**mirroring is its native condition, not a decoration** (ATTESTED). The
flame resemblance itself is HYPOTHESIS, testable by the same Experiment
002. The consonantal root system means the sound carrier works
differently there (vowels are diacritic, often unwritten) — a boundary
to state before any mapping is attempted.

## VI. Verdict

**Yes — it helps, and the help has edges.** ABC-as-pitch-bank plus
Braille-as-segment-bank demonstrably maps the meaning-bearing pitch of
Japanese (triplet separated), the fallback's exact failure line is found
(pitch), the extension to tonal Asian languages follows a checkable rule,
and the Sanskrit/Arabic proposals each contain one attested structural
bridge (articulation order; positional allography under native mirroring)
and one visual hypothesis awaiting Experiment 002. Filed as a research
attempt under the 2026-10-09 ruling: demonstrated where demonstrated,
hypothesis where hypothesis, nothing decreed.
