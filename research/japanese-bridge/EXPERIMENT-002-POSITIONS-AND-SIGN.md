# EXPERIMENT 002 — POSITIONS AND SIGN
## The Sanskrit body-position question, and sign as a bridge to a bank

**Ordered by Johnathan "Qasparr" Monroe, 2026-10-28:** "does the Kama
Svtra positions as alphabet find any hits?" and "can maybe use sign as a
bridge to a bank if yes." Follow-up to Experiment 001 (the sound carrier).
Same law: demonstrated or not; no bridge decreed; tiers labeled.

## I. The Kama Sutra question — one miss, two hits

**The named source: NO HIT.** Nothing found ties alphabet-to-body-position
mapping to the Kāma Sūtra of Vātsyāyana itself. The desk does not
fabricate the link; the attribution, as named, does not verify.

**Hit 1 — the system exists, under its own name: Mātṛkānyāsa (ATTESTED).**
The Sanskrit tradition itself maps the alphabet onto body positions:
Matrika-nyasa, "installation of the Mothers," described in the Devi Gita
(part of the Devi Bhagavata Purana) and discussed in the Tantrarāja-Tantra
and Kulacūḍāmaṇi, installs the Matrikas — as letters of the alphabet — in
the worshipper's body, part by part (head, face, legs, and the rest),
with mantras. A modern summary counts **the 50 letters of the Sanskrit
alphabet installed on different parts of the body**. The author's
intuition — *the alphabet as positions of the body* — locates a real,
documented system; it lives in Tantra, not in the Kāma Sūtra.

**Hit 2 — the glyph test (DEMONSTRATED here, first form).** The
varṇamālā groups the 25 consonants by articulation position (varga):
velar (throat), palatal, retroflex, dental (teeth), labial (lips) — five
letters each. Test: if position shapes form, letters made at the same
body position should LOOK more alike than letters made at different
positions. Method: Noto Sans Devanagari glyphs rendered, pixel-vector
cosine similarity over all 300 pairs; permutation null, 2,000 shuffles.

- Mean similarity WITHIN a position: **0.6587** (50 pairs)
- Mean similarity BETWEEN positions: **0.6188** (250 pairs)
- Difference **+0.0399**, permutation **p = 0.015**

**Verdict on the test: a real but small signal.** Same-position letters
are measurably more alike than chance allows — the body's positions leave
a trace in the letterforms. Zero points, stated: one typeface; the shared
headline (śirorekhā) inflates all similarities (the high base), though it
inflates both sides equally; and the test measures *grouping by
articulation position*, NOT resemblance of glyphs to body postures —
that further claim remains untested, and nothing here is evidence for it.

## II. Sign as a bridge to a bank — YES, attested and already built

**Yubimoji (指文字, "finger letters")** — the Japanese manual syllabary,
part of Japanese Sign Language: **45 signs and 4 diacritics representing
the phonetic syllables of Japanese**, built on the ASL manual alphabet.
Signs are distinguished by handshape, by **direction**, and by **palm
orientation** — na, ni, ha share one handshape and differ by direction
alone: form change by orientation, in the hand.

So sign is not a candidate bank awaiting invention; it is a working bank
with a literature. For Japanese the bank set is now threefold and
complete across the senses:

| Channel (sense) | Bank | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| Ear (hearing) | ABC notation | pitch — the meaning-bearing flow (Exp. 001) |
| Hand (touch) | Tenji (Japanese Braille) | the syllable skeleton |
| Eye (sight) | Yubimoji (manual kana) | handshape + direction + orientation |

Exp. 001's boundary stands inside the triad: kanji semantics still rides
none of the three (kūsho — air-writing kanji — is JSL's own patch for
exactly that gap, noted, not tested).

## III. Standing after Experiments 001–002

- Japanese: bridge ATTESTED in three banks (phonology complete; semantics
  open). No values in any weighing bank are implied — these are encodings;
  a Japanese *weighing* bank remains a separate ruling.
- Sanskrit: body-position bridge ATTESTED as system (nyāsa) and as a
  small glyph signal (p = 0.015); Kama Sutra attribution NOT verified;
  posture-resemblance UNTESTED.
- The method (vector form + mirroring vs a null) has now produced one
  measured result; the Arabic flame question can be run on the same bench
  whenever ordered.
