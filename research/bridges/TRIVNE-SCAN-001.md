## Braille (hand) · ABC/audio (ear) · Sign (eye) — the hybrid bridge census

**Ordered by Johnathan "Qasparr" Monroe, 2026-10-28:** "finally scan all
languages for braille, abc, sign Trivne hybrid bridges." Scope: the
twelve-language Asian program set under the [#N] folder registry. Tiers

## Findings

**1. THE HAND NEVER FAILS — 12 of 12.** Every language in the set has a
working Braille. But the zero point inside the victory: the codes are
not one system. They fall into lineages that mirror the script families —
the Bharati (Indian) lineage covers Devanagari and the Indic scripts;
the Russian lineage carries Mongolian; French-ordered codes carry
Vietnamese and Cantonese; Japanese, Korean, and the Chinese schemes are
independent semi-syllabaries; Thai-and-Lao share one listing; Burmese,
Khmer, and Tibetan stand as their own scripts. The touch bank is
universal, but it is a family tree, not a single key.

**2. THE EAR IS THE VARIABLE BANK.** Full tone/pitch seats: Mandarin (4),
Cantonese (6–9), Vietnamese (6), Thai (5), Lao (5–6), Burmese
(tone-register), modern Tibetan (tones innovated from old consonant
clusters — classical Tibetan had none; the boundary is dated by the
language's own history), Japanese (pitch accent, Exp. 001). Partial:
Sanskrit (Vedic udātta/anudātta/svarita accent — real, historical;
modern Hindi non-tonal), Korean (Seoul non-tonal; pitch accent lives in
Gyeongsang dialect; the engine seat is acoustic features — VOT,
aspiration, tension — per the hybrid design). Register only: Khmer
(breathy/modal, not tone). Absent: Mongolian.

**3. THE EYE IS EVERYWHERE, UNEVENLY BANKED.** Manual letter systems are
attested for all twelve, but only some are syllabary-grade banks:
- **Japanese yubimoji** — 45 signs + 4 diacritics (Exp. 002).
- **Korean jimunja (지문자)** — VERIFIED this scan: a one-handed manual
  alphabet that maps directly onto Hangul jamo, and its handshapes
  *mimic the shapes of the Hangul letters themselves* (the bent index for
  ㄱ). The sign bank traces the glyph bank — a form-bridge inside a
  single language, the Korean twin of the Sanskrit glyph result
  (Exp. 002, p = 0.015).
- **Chinese fingerspelling scheme** — VERIFIED: adopted December 1963,
  one-handed, pinyin-based, with ZH/CH/SH/NG as single signs because
  they are single phonemes — the hand bank respects the sound bank's
  own units.
- **Indian Sign Language** — two-handed fingerspelling (British-derived),
  the Indic outlier in hand-count.
- Tibetan Sign Language exists as an isolate; its manual letter bank is
  thin in the record — marked partial, not absent.

**4. TIBETAN BRAILLE CARRIES THE VARGA.** VERIFIED this scan: Tibetan
Braille (Sabriye Tenberken, 1992; official system for the blind in
Tibet) writes Tibetan *ka, kha, ga, nga* with the Braille letters for
*k, c, g* and lowered *g* — the Sanskrit throat-row seated in the hand
bank of Tibet. The pivot grid travels.

## Verdict — where the Trivne stands complete

- **Complete (3/3 strong):** Japanese, Mandarin, Vietnamese, Thai.
- **Complete with one marked caveat:** Sanskrit/Hindi (ear = historical
  accent), Korean (ear = acoustic engine, not tone).
- **Two strong + one partial:** Cantonese, Lao, Burmese, Tibetan.
- **The hybrid engine's true work-list:** Khmer (register engine),
  Mongolian (acoustic engine; sign bank thin) — here the Sanskrit-pivot
  hybrid earns its keep, because no ready-made tone bank exists to copy.

The pattern across all twelve: **the hand bank is universal, the eye
bank is common, the ear bank is earned language by language.** That is
the shape of the remaining work.
