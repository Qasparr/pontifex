## II. THE SEVEN RULINGS

### G1 — THE AXIS: gojūon positional, read as the dialect reads its own table

**The gap** (Phase I §VI.1, §VI.2, §VI.10): which sequence
seats the values; in what order the vowels run inside each
row; which signs exist in the inventory.

**The evidence.** Two living candidates for an order stand
in the field: the gojūon grid (Fukada's order, and the
dialect's own — Abroad III §II seats あ い う え お as the
dialect's canon, its order attested) and the Utahi sequence
(Yoshino's — a poem's sound-list, whose inventory cannot
weigh ク or ヘ at all). The iroha order exists in the culture
with no bank on it. For the column order, the field holds
exactly one systematic deviation from the schoolbook: Fukada
reads each row in the column order **a-o-u-e-i**, his
school's fingerprint (Phase I §II.1).

**The ruling [PROPOSED].** The bank is **gojūon positional**:
values are sequence positions in the gojūonzu grid, read
**row-major in the spoken column order a-i-u-e-o** — the
order the dialect itself speaks its canon in:

> あ=1, い=2, う=3, え=4, お=5, か=6, き=7, く=8, け=9,
> こ=10, さ=11 … を=47, ん=48; the voiced block follows at
> 49–73 under G2.

*Reasoning.* A positional bank is its reading order and
nothing else; the only order with a claim on a Japanese
bank is the order the dialect's own table is read in.
あ い う え お is not the desk's preference — it is the
head row of the gojūon as the dialect hands it down
(ATTESTED as the table's order, Abroad III §II), and Rule 6
of that essay — *order before number: a dialect is first
weighed in its own ruled order* — is the doctrine the mint
obeys. Fukada's a-o-u-e-i is another founder's deviation,
his fingerprint; a mint that inherited it would carry his
hand in every value it ever printed. The mint declines the
inheritance and prints the coordinate instead:

> **The permutation coordinate.** The mint and Fukada read
> the same grid. Within every full row, the two readings
> differ by exactly one transposition: the **i- and
> o-columns swap offsets** (mint: a=0, i=1, u=2, e=3, o=4;
> Fukada: a=0, o=1, u=2, e=3, i=4). The a-, u-, and
> e-columns hold the same within-row offset in both banks.
> Where rows have holes （や・わ）, the absolute seats drift
> further; §III prints both banks against every sign, so
> any reader can measure any sign's Δ for himself. Neither
> reading is the other's error: they are two declared
> traversals of one grid, and the Δ between them is a
> coordinate, not a verdict.

**The inventory [PROPOSED].** The grid seats **47 signs**:
the modern syllabary's grid signs, with the や row's *yi*
and *ye* cells and the わ row's *wu* cell standing as
**holes** — no kana, no seat, printed as vacancies in §III
(the grid holds their places; the bank seats no ghosts).
**ゐ (wi, seat 45) and ゑ (we, seat 46) are seated as
ARCHIVAL seats** — the dialect's own digamma (Abroad III
§III.5): struck from the living table in 1946, standing in
old texts and names, held here under the house's disposition
law, *marked, never vanished*. **を takes its grid seat,
47** — the seat kept though its sound surrendered to お
(§III.5's inverse ghost). **ん, the hatsuon, stands outside
the fifty grid** — one beat, no vowel, no cell (Abroad III
§II) — and takes **the next seat after the grid: 48. ONE
value.** The field's one dual seat — Fukada's ン, 1 if
visible, 10 if not — is **refused**: a sign with two values
is a fork in every sum it enters, and a bank whose totals
can be elected after the fact cannot be audited. The mint's
ン weighs 48 wherever it stands. (Its coordinate against
the field is printed in §III: Fukada 1-or-10; Yoshino 48 —
the mint's seat agrees with Yoshino's at this sign, by the
different road of the grid's end rather than a poem's.)

**The dresses.** Hiragana and katakana are one sign in two
dresses (Abroad III, Rule 7) and share every seat: あ and
ア are one entry of this bank, by construction in the
engine and by statement here.

### G2 — THE DAKUTEN LAW: continuation, on the mint's own axis

**The gap** (Phase I §VI.3, §VI.4): what voicing does to a
value; what the handakuten does. The field's widest
mechanical divergence: Fukada continues his sequence
(51–75); Yoshino subtracts (a voiced sound counts as its
clear sound's negative — ガ = −25, a law that drives a
divine name to −1 in the test matrix); for handakuten
Fukada continues （パ row 71–75) while Yoshino adds the
base （パ = +42).

**The derivation, attempted and printed.** The desk first
sought the grid's own answer — rule (c): *a dakuten sign
weighs its base sign's seat plus a constant derived from
the grid's structure.* Two derivations were tried. (i)
*The mark-count:* the dakuten is two dots, the handakuten
one circle — constants +2 and +1. Rejected: it prices the
pen-strokes of a diacritic, a fact about writing the mark,
not about the grid; no house bank prices a sign by its
strokes, and the constant would be a pun wearing a proof.
(ii) *The column-count:* the grid is five columns wide, so
voicing displaces by 5. Rejected: the width is a fact of
the grid, but nothing in the grid's structure assigns the
voicing a five-step displacement rather than any other;
the 5 is chosen, not derived. A third form, the limiting
case *same seat* (constant 0 — the voiced sign weighs as
its base), was rejected as a ruling: it makes the
diacritic free, erasing a distinction the script declares
on its face — and this bank's own G3 honours what spelling
declares. **No derivation holds.** The desk says so, and
takes the fallback the evidence supports.

**The ruling [PROPOSED].** The voiced rows take **the next
seats after the base inventory entire, in the grid's
printed dakuten order**:

> が行 49–53 · ざ行 54–58 · だ行 59–63 · ば行 64–68 ·
> ぱ行 69–73.

*Reasoning.* Continuation is the shape of the field's one
complete precedent (Fukada), it keeps every total positive
and every sign singly valued, and it treats voicing the
way the script treats it — as a register added to the
inventory, printed after the clear signs in every school
chart. But the seats are **the mint's own**: Fukada's
continuation begins at 51 because his reading seats fifty
cells (he fills the yi/ye/wu holes with archaic slots);
the mint's begins at 49 because its inventory is 47 grid
seats + ん. His が is 51; the mint's が is 49. The shape is
his precedent's; the axis is the founder's project's own,
and the Δ is printed wherever the two banks meet (§III,
§V). Subtraction is refused for the reason Phase I's
matrix exhibits: a bank that can weigh イザナミ at −1 has
let a mechanic outrank the arithmetic's dignity — house
totals are counts, and counts do not go negative. The
handakuten follows the dakuten in the same block (the
printed order が・ざ・だ・ば・ぱ is the charts' own),
against Yoshino's addition rule, for the same reason her
subtraction is refused: the mint seats signs; it does not
operate on them.

### G3 — THE CHŌON LAW: the mark weighs the vowel it lengthens

**The gap** (Phase I §VI.6): what a long vowel is worth.
The field: silence (Fukada, the academy) or zero (Yoshino:
ー counts 0). Abroad III already seats the long vowel at
the structural tier as the dialect's own OFFSET — +1 mora,
constant, declared in the spelling (§III.2, Rule 3: *length
is an offset, never a seat*).

**The ruling [PROPOSED].** The mark ー is **an operator,
not a sign**: it weighs **the seat value of the vowel it
lengthens** — a second mora *of that vowel*. かー weighs
カ 6 + あ 1; とー weighs ト 20 + お 5; きー weighs キ 7 +
い 2. A ー with no vowel before it (word-initial, or after
ん・っ, which carry no vowel) weighs 0.

*Reasoning.* Abroad III's classification is the ruling's
doctrine: the chōon is the pure vowel plus a constant the
spelling declares, and the constant is not a number of
nothing — it is *one more mora of the same quality*. To
weigh ー at 0 (Yoshino) erases exactly what the spelling
declares: the length is in the word, sounded and timed,
and a bank that zeroes it weighs a shorter word than the
one written. To seat ー as a sign of its own would crown
length as a seat, against Rule 3. The operator reading is
the only one that honours both the offset doctrine and
the mora: the lengthened vowel is weighed twice because
it *is* sounded twice — two morae, one quality.

**The seam this opens, printed.** Japanese also writes
length with a vowel kana — おう, おお, えい, ええ
(Abroad III §III.2–3) — and there the bank's fence (G6:
the written sign) governs: the signs weigh their seats,
おう = お 5 + う 3 = **8**, where the length-reading of
the same spelling would give お 5 + お 5 = **10** (Δ2,
printed). えい weighs え 4 + い 2 = **6** as written. The
desk prints the divergence rather than legislating past
it, for a stated reason: the mark ー has no office but
length, so its operator reading is unambiguous; a vowel
kana in second position is *sometimes* length and
sometimes a true second mora across a morpheme boundary
（思う *omou* sounds its う whole), and a sign bank cannot
read morphology. Where the script declares length with
its dedicated sign, the mint honours the declaration at
full value; where length hides inside a vowel letter, the
mint weighs the letter and prints the seam. [The seam is
recorded as a coordinate of this proposal; a future ruling
may close it, and if it does, the totals that move are
exactly the long-vowel spellings, enumerable on demand.]

### G4 — THE YŌON LAW: the small letter weighs whole

**The gap** (Phase I §VI.7): what a contracted sound is
worth. Yoshino alone rules: small ャ ュ ョ at full-size
value. Fukada's silence leaves his bank unable, in open
sources, to weigh キャ at all.

**The ruling [PROPOSED].** **A small kana weighs at its
full-size form's seat**: ゃ = ヤ 36, ゅ = ユ 37, ょ = ヨ 38;
the small vowels ァ ィ ゥ ェ ォ at あ い う え お's seats
(1–5); and by the same law extended to the small forms the
modern script still writes, ゎ = ワ 44, ゕ = カ 6, ゖ = ケ 9.

*Reasoning.* The yōon mora is one mora, sounded whole: きゃ
is a single beat whose glide is fully pronounced — the
smallness is in the *letterform*, and Abroad III §III.1
reads that smallness as the script's confession of fusion,
not of diminution: two signs, one beat, neither sign
discounted. A bank that halved or zeroed the small letter
would weigh the confession instead of the sound. Yoshino's
full-value rule is the precedent, and the mora argument is
the reasoning; the extension to ゎ・ゕ・ゖ is the same law
at the inventory's edge, marked as the desk's extension of
it. (The sokuon is *not* seated by this law but by G5 —
its seat is a separate ruling, though the value coincides
with its full-size form's.)

### G5 — THE SOKUON LAW: the stopped beat weighs as ツ

**The gap** (Phase I §VI.8): what the geminate mark is
worth. Yoshino: ッ = ツ (44, her seat). The others: silence.

**The ruling [PROPOSED].** **っ/ッ weighs at つ/ツ's seat:
18.**

*Reasoning.* The sokuon is a full mora of blockage — the
beat is *kept*, the breath stopped in it (Abroad III's
ghost rule is its kin: timing stands where voice is
withdrawn). It is not silence and must not weigh as
silence. Its full-size form is ツ — the small つ is the
glyph's own confession of what it is — and Yoshino's rule
is the field's one precedent. The mint's seat differs from
hers (18 against her 44) because the axis differs; the
*law* — the mark takes its full-size self's value — is the
same law, and the agreement of law across rival axes is
printed as the coordinate it is.

### G6 — THE DEVOICING QUESTION: the bank weighs the written sign

**The gap** (Phase I §VI.9): does the mint weigh the
written sign or the sounded mora? No predecessor bank
rules it; all three weigh the orthography. Abroad III's
fourth hybrid operation is precisely the devoiced vowel —
the sound present and absent at once (§III.4).

**The ruling [PROPOSED].** **The bank weighs the written
sign.** すき weighs す 13 + き 7 = **20**, its う-seat
inside す counted whole, though Tokyo whispers that vowel
to a ghost.

*Reasoning, and the fence declared.* A gematria bank is an
instrument of the written record: it must return the same
total for the same spelling in every mouth, or it is a
dialect survey, not a bank. Devoicing is real — attested
phonology, a seat of the dialect's hybrid machinery — and
it belongs to the **performance register**, where the
house already keeps it: the Divine Nine vocal tool is the
instrument that prices sounded vowels, ghosts included.
The mint's fence is therefore declared, not silent: *this
bank's jurisdiction is the sign; the ghost seat stands in
the vocal tool's, and the two instruments' readings of a
devoiced word will differ by exactly the ghost's seat —
a coordinate any future audit can compute.* All three
predecessors are orthographic; every house bank is
orthographic; the mint is orthographic, and says so on
its face.

### G7 — THE REDUCTION LAW: totals stand raw

**The gap** (Phase I §VI.11): do totals stand raw, or
reduce, and by what published law? Fukada: raw, read
against a dictionary of totals. Yoshino: digit-reduction
by law, 10 retained. The academy: reduction by a method
it does not publish — the field's one sealed mechanic,
and Phase I's standing exhibit of what the house method

**The ruling [PROPOSED].** **Totals stand raw.** A
digit-sum reduction may be printed *beside* a total as a
reading, never *instead* of it — the rōmaji seam's Rule 7,
carried over whole, with its reasoning: a reduction may be
recorded as resonance at [DERIVED]; it may not replace,
correct, or outrank the raw total it reduces. And the
refusal that completes this ruling is G1's: **no dual
values anywhere in the bank** — no sign elects its value
by context, visibility, or the weigher's need. A bank
whose laws can all be read is a bank that can be audited;
that, and not antiquity, is the dignity this mint claims.
