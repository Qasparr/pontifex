### III.4 — The mechanics, compared

Each bank's ordering principle and working rules, at the
coordinates the open record states them:

**Order.** Fukada: the gojūon grid, rows in the schoolbook
order, vowel columns re-ordered **a-o-u-e-i** — a grid-order,
chosen and systematic. Yoshino: the **Utahi 5–6 sequence** —
a text-order; the table is a poem's sound-list numbered.
Yamato-shiki: at every exercised point, Fukada's grid-order;
the academy names its own foundation the 元数盤， the 369
magic square (its page), a basis it shares in words with the
Fukada line's own 9×9 magic-square framing (41 at the centre)
— the framing descends with the table.

**Dakuten law — the field's widest mechanical divergence.**
Fukada (and the academy at its exercised points): voiced
sounds are **the sequence continued** — the dakuten rows take
51–75 in gojūon order, which is to say each voiced sound's
value is its own seat, 45 past its row's head. Yoshino:
voiced sounds are **subtracted** — the official rule is
printed in her 数霊計算の法則， item 4: 濁音は引き算にする，
and her practitioners expound it as: a voiced sound counts
as the *negative* of its clear sound's value （ガ = −カ =
−25). One language, two opposite laws: in the first bank a
dakuten *adds a register*; in the second it *reverses a
sign*.

**Handakuten law.** Fukada: continuation, like the dakuten
（パ row at 71–75). Yoshino: the mirror of her dakuten rule —
handakuten sounds **add** their base's value （パ = +ハ =
+42), per her practitioners' exposition of the official
rules [practitioner tier].

**The moraic nasal.** Fukada: the field's only
context-dependent value — ン = 1 or 10 by a semantic rule
(visible / invisible), the rule expounded from the school's
own 2009 volume. Yoshino: ン = 48, fixed, the last seat of
the poem. The academy: ン = 1 at both its exercised points,
no rule stated on the page.

**Long vowel (chōon).** Yoshino rules it: the mark ー counts
**0** — excluded from the sum (official-site rule list via
her practitioners; her 思念 rules likewise treat the
lengthening sound as its own, non-numeric category).
Native long vowels written with vowel kana are, in all three
banks, weighed as the signs written. Fukada and the academy:
**no rule for the ー mark in open sources** — a silence,
printed as one.

**Yōon.** Yoshino rules it: small ャ ュ ョ count at their
**full-size values** （キャ = キ 29 + ヤ 15, per her
practitioners' worked rule). Fukada and the academy: **no
rule in open sources**; the small kana carry no values in
Fukada's table.

**Sokuon.** Yoshino rules it: ッ counts as **ツ** (44).
Fukada and the academy: **no rule in open sources**.

**Devoicing.** No bank in the open record addresses it. All
three banks are **orthographic**: they weigh the sign written,
whether or not the sounded mora devoices (the [ɯ] and [i]
that Japanese drops between voiceless consonants are weighed
at full value as ウ and イ）. The Japanese Dialect essay's
fourth hybrid operation — the devoiced vowel, present and
absent at once — has no mechanic in any living bank. The mint
will have to rule it from nothing (§VI).

**Reduction of totals.** Fukada: **none** — the total stands
raw and is read against the school's dictionary of totals
(the 決定版's dictionary runs 1–181; totals past it occur in
practitioner material and are read by further machinery of
the school, beyond the base bank). Yoshino: **digit-reduction
is law** — her rule 3: every total is reduced to one digit
（次元数， "dimensional number"), with **10 retained**
unreduced beside 1–9; equal dimensional numbers share
attributes. Yamato-shiki: reduction exists and is
**proprietary** — the academy's page states its sums are
brought to a single digit "by our own calculation method"
and teaches the method inside the school: **the law's
existence is published; the law is NOT IN OPEN SOURCES**.

## IV. THE SHOWPIECE TEST MATRIX

Every worked equation the research carried is here tested
against **each** bank's sourced table, with the sums stated
stepwise. *Holds* = the bank's own values produce the claimed
total. *Fails* = they produce another. *Cannot-test* = a sign
the word needs is absent from that bank as published in open
sources. A claim tested against its own bank is marked for
what it is: a bank cannot fail its own exhibit unless its
printed arithmetic is wrong — the independent tests are the
cross-bank rows.

## VI. THE PHASE III EXTRACTION — THE GAPS THE MINT MUST RULE

Phase I's purpose was to lay the field's evidence before the
founder's rulings. The gaps below are stated as questions.
Each carries what the three banks do, as the evidence; no gap
is answered here, because answering them is the mint, and the
mint is the founder's pen under the Flagpole.

1. **THE ORDER QUESTION.** *Which sequence seats the
values?* The field offers two living candidates: the
gojūon grid (Fukada's order — and the dialect's own, per
Abroad III) and the Utahi sequence (Yoshino's). A third
order, the iroha, exists in the culture with no bank on
it. The mint's first ruling founds all the others.
2. **THE VOWEL-COLUMN QUESTION.** *In what order do the
vowels run inside each row?* Fukada's a-o-u-e-i is the
one systematic deviation from the schoolbook a-i-u-e-o in
the field; the mint must rule its own column order, and
the ruling moves every value in the table.
3. **THE DAKUTEN LAW.** *What does voicing do to a value?*
The field's widest divergence: continuation (Fukada —
the voiced rows take the next seats, 51–75) against
subtraction (Yoshino — the voiced sound counts as its
clear sound's negative). A third road — voiced sounds
unvalued, or valued by their own new principle — is open
and unclaimed by any bank.
4. **THE HANDAKUTEN LAW.** Continuation (Fukada, パ row at
71–75) against addition of the base (Yoshino, パ = +ハ).
Ruled with the dakuten law or against it — the field
shows both symmetries.
5. **THE ン LAW.** *May a sign carry two values?* Fukada's
dual ン (1 visible / 10 invisible) is the field's only
context-dependent seat; Yoshino's is fixed at 48, last.
The mint must rule whether its table admits a dual seat
at all — and if not, where the moraic nasal sits.
6. **THE CHŌON LAW.** *What is a long vowel worth?* The
banks are silent (Fukada, the academy) or zero it
(Yoshino, ー = 0). Abroad III already seats the long
vowel as the dialect's own OFFSET (+1 mora, constant and
declared) at the structural tier; whether the mint's
numbers honour that offset — a long vowel as its vowel
twice, as its vowel plus a constant, or as zero — is a
ruling the field leaves entirely open.
7. **THE YŌON LAW.** *What is a contracted sound worth?*
Yoshino alone rules: small ャ ュ ョ at full-size value
(two seats per mora). Fukada's silence leaves his bank
unable, in open sources, to weigh キャ at all. The mint
must choose: full value, no value, or a fused single
seat for the contraction.
8. **THE SOKUON LAW.** *What is the geminate mark worth?*
Yoshino: ッ = ツ (44) — the mark takes its full-size
self's value. The others: silence. Ruled with the yōon
law or separately.
9. **THE DEVOICING QUESTION.** *Does the mint weigh the
written sign or the sounded mora?* No bank rules it; all
three weigh the orthography. But the dialect's fourth
hybrid operation (Abroad III) is precisely the devoiced
vowel — the sound that is present and absent at once.
A mint that weighs sounds must price it; a mint that
weighs signs must say so, and the saying is a ruling.
10. **THE INVENTORY QUESTION.** *Which signs exist in the
mint's syllabary?* Fukada seats 75 + ン， including five
archaic slots (ye, yi, wu, we, wi) no living word uses;
Yoshino seats 48 and cannot weigh ヘ. The mint must fix
its inventory — 46, 48, 50, 75 — and rule the archaic
slots and the ヰ・ヱ seats expressly.
11. **THE REDUCTION LAW.** *Do totals stand raw, or reduce,
and by what published law?* Fukada: raw, read against a
dictionary of totals (1–181). Yoshino: digit-reduction
with 10 retained. The academy: reduction by a method it
does not publish — the field's one sealed mechanic, and
a standing exhibit of what the house's method forbids:
a law that cannot be read cannot be audited.
12. **THE MEANING QUESTION.** *Do the mint's totals carry
meanings, or values only?* Fukada's bank is inseparable
in practice from its dictionary of what each total
means; the house's doctrine (reasoning inline on every
correspondence; values prove themselves at the
Anchorage) would seat meanings, if at all, as a further
instrument under its own audit. The mint rules numbers
first; whether a dictionary follows is its own later
question, stated here so it is not smuggled.
