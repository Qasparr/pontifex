## I. THE COMMISSION AND THE PHASE

The founder ruled the three roads of this project in one
breath — *"Do all 3 but in order that is a synergy not an
argument"* — and the order is: **Phase I**, the audit of the
three modern banks (complete: the field established entire,
two tables where three were advertised, and the twelve gaps
the mint must rule, extracted at its §VI); **Phase II**,
this instrument — the rōmaji seam, formalized; **Phase III**,
the gojūon mint, the founder's own kana bank, coined under
the Flagpole and not yet begun.

**Why the seam needs a phase of its own.** Every Japanese
number the house has ever carried entered by one door: a
Japanese word in its roman dress, weighed in the house's
Latin banks. That door was opened by Abroad III §VII, used
by the Mugetsu-Gumi charter, the Hellverse treatment, and
Essay IV's audit — but it was never *formalized*: its
weighings stand scattered across four documents, its rules
stand implicit in its practice, and its limits stand stated
in one essay only. A mint coined against an unstated
standard cannot be audited against it. This phase therefore
does three things and no more: it **gathers** every rōmaji
weighing of record into one inventory and recomputes each
on the house engine (§III); it **states the seam's rules**
as the corpus's practice exhibits them, with the exhibits
cited (§IV) and the calibration corpus weighed entire
(§V); and it **sets the protocol** the Phase III mint must
answer to (§VI).

**The rails of this phase.** (1) *Recompute, never trust.*

## II. THE SEAM DEFINED

**The seam is this:** a Japanese word, written in rōmaji —
its Latin dress — is weighed in the house's Latin banks
(English ordinal A=1…Z=26; Agrippa's table), and the result
is carried as a house number *of the dress*, at the declared

**What it is for.** The bridge doctrine of the house: each
word is weighed in its own tongue, and the purpose is the
bridge — the shared number across honest banks. Japanese,
until Phase III, has no house bank of its own; what it has
is a dress the house's banks can read. The seam is the only
road by which Japanese material has ever entered the house's

The founder's Japanese coinages — Akuma no mai Mugetsu, the
Mugetsu-Gumi, MVRRAMASSA ZANGETSV — all stand in the house
record *as seam-weighings*. There is no other Japanese
arithmetic in the house. That is the seam's dignity and its
limit in one statement.

**Its declared limits, printed at the same size.**

1. *It weighs the dress, not the kana.* AKUMA 47 is a fact
   about the Latin letters A-K-U-M-A. It asserts nothing
   about あくま, and the governing refusal of Abroad III
   (no attested kana bank) is untouched by every number in
   this document — as Essay IV's correctio left it: narrowed
   to history, standing.
2. *It is a bridge, not the far shore.* A bridge is judged
   by whether it bears weight and lands honestly, not by
   whether it resembles the country it reaches. The seam's
   numbers are real numbers; they are not Japanese numbers,
   and Phase III exists precisely because the house refuses
   to mistake the one for the other.
3. *It is register-sensitive.* The same word in the
   founder's inscriptional dress (AKVMA, MVGETSV) weighs
   differently in the ordinal bank (§IV, Rule 4). A seam
   weighing that does not declare its dress is incomplete.

## IV. THE SEAM'S RULES — FORMALIZED FROM THE CORPUS'S PRACTICE

No rule below is invented here. Each is stated in the form
the corpus already obeys, with its exhibit; where the
corpus's practice runs out, the gap is printed as an OPEN
COORDINATE and carried to Phase III (§VI), not legislated
silently in a phase whose commission is to formalize, not
to found.

**Rule 1 — Romanization: Hepburn, weighed as letters.**
The corpus's dress is Hepburn (Abroad III §VII, declared):
Kunrei-shiki is named there and not used. The seam weighs
*letters, not sounds*: Hepburn's digraphs — SH, CH, TS —
enter the banks as the Latin letters that spell them
(SHION 65/197 turns on S+H; MUGETSU's TS on T+S), and the
EI collapse is weighed as the letters E+I = 14, not as the
sound [eː] it collapses to. *Reasoning:* the banks read
graphemes; a seam that weighed phonemes would need a
phoneme bank, which is Phase III's question, not this
phase's instrument. *Sub-coordinate, open:* Hepburn writes
the moraic nasal ん as **n**, save **m** before b, p, m in
the traditional form. The corpus's only ん-exhibit is
word-final (SHION) — the m-variant is unattested in the
corpus and stands as an open coordinate for any future
weighing of a labial-following nasal.

**Rule 2 — Length unspelled: the default side of the seam.**
Long vowels are weighed unspelled: the macron (ō) and the
Kunrei circumflex (ô) belong to neither Latin bank, so the
declared dress drops them — Tōsō → **TOSO** (Abroad III
§VII, the seam's own zero point). Every §VII value of
record holds at this side of the seam, and any weighing
taken at the other side must say so on its face.

**Rule 3 — The spelled-length offset: constant within a
bank, differing across banks.** When length *is* spelled,

Gate item 7): OFFSET at a declared seam, the seam being

U/V-seam doctrine (QVANTVM against QUANTUM)
wearing rōmaji. *Open coordinate:* the **oo-dress**
(TOSOO) is unattested in the corpus — the corpus exhibits
exactly two dresses of a long vowel, unspelled and
ou-spelled. Which dress a Phase III seam twin takes for a
long-vowel word — and whether the twin's dress follows the
mint's own chōon ruling (Phase I gap 6) — is left open at

## VI. THE PHASE III PROTOCOL — HOW THE MINT ANSWERS TO THE SEAM

Phase III coins a kana bank under the Flagpole. This
section binds nothing about the mint's values — the twelve
gaps of Phase I §VI are the founder's to rule, and this
phase answers none of them. What it binds is the mint's
*accounting*: how a kana weighing is reported so that it
can be read against the standard that already carries the
house's Japanese doctrine.

**P1 — Every kana weighing ships with a seam twin.** Any
word weighed in the Phase III bank is also weighed in
rōmaji under §IV's rules (Hepburn, length unspelled unless
declared, plain dress unless the name is inscribed — and
for inscribed names, both dresses, per Rule 4), in both
Latin banks, and the twin is printed beside the kana
weighing. *Reasoning:* the seam is the only existing
reader of Japanese material in the house; a kana total
with no twin cannot be compared to anything the house
already holds, and an incomparable total is an orphan.

**P2 — A bridge is a named exhibit, never a mood.** A
**BRIDGE** is recorded when, for a named word, the kana
bank's total and the seam twin's ordinal total agree
exactly. Bridges are entered one by one, with the word,
the bank, and the twin printed; a bridge claimed for a
class of words from one exhibit is a misprint. *No bridge
is promised by this protocol, and none is expected as a
class:* the banks are different instruments — one counts
Latin letters by position and by Agrippa's seats, the
other will count mora-signs by the founder's ruling — and
agreement, where it emerges, is an event, not a design
requirement. If the mint's あくま lands on 47, that is a
bridge of the first water and will be printed at that
size; if it does not, P3 governs.

**P3 — Divergence is printed as coordinates, never as
failure.** Where kana total and seam twin differ, the Δ is
printed with its coordinate (word; kana bank; seam bank;
dress), exactly as the seam prints its own internal
offsets (TOSO/TOUSOU, Δ42/Δ400; EI/ΕΙ, Δ1). The zero-point
doctrine of the house governs: report the boundary
coordinates where instruments diverge instead of a verdict
blob. A mint that diverged from the seam everywhere would
not thereby fail; a mint that *hid* its divergences would
fail the Method of Science on its face.

**P4 — The seam remains the calibration standard.** The
mint must not orphan the doctrine the seam carries. At its
founding, the mint prints its own あくま beside the seam's
**AKUMA 47**, its むげつ beside **MUGETSU 106**, and its
readings of the §V calibration corpus beside the twins —
whether they agree or not. *Reasoning:* AKUMA 47 is
load-bearing beyond this project: it is a seat of the 47
Trinity, the calibration witness of two issued essays
(Abroad III §VII.1; Essay IV's audit calibration), and the
anchor exhibit of the Divine Nine vocal tool's Japanese
seat-row. Doctrine built on the seam's numbers does not
fall if the mint diverges — the seam stands as its own
record, at DERIVED, forever — but the mint owes the record
a printed reading against it, so that no future audit has
to guess where the two instruments part.

**P5 — The open coordinates this phase hands to Phase
III**, gathered in one place:

1. *The spelled-length dress of seam twins* (Rule 3):
   unspelled (the corpus default), ou, or oo — and whether
   the twin's dress follows the mint's chōon ruling
   (Phase I gap 6) or stands independent of it.
2. *The labial nasal* (Rule 1): Hepburn's ん → m before
   b, p, m is unattested in the corpus; the mint's twin
   practice must declare itself at its first such word.
3. *Inscriptional twins in Agrippa* (Rule 4): a VV in
   Agrippa is not a respelling but a voicing (W silent →
   V+V at 200 each); any Phase III comparison that crosses
   into inscriptional dress must name its bank before its
   Δ.
4. *The mint's own twelve* (Phase I §VI, carried whole):
   order; vowel-column order; dakuten law; handakuten law;
