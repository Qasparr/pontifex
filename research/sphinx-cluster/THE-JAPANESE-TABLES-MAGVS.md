## I. THE FRONT GATE — THE REFUSAL AS NARROWED

This volume opens where the project opened: at a refusal,
and at the founder's refusal to let a refusal stand
unexamined. The Divine Nine Abroad III, the Japanese

itself: the Milesian values of the Greek bank do not
transfer to kana — no seam, no constant, no mirror — and
any claim of attested numeric correspondence between kana
and number FAILS. The founder carried that refusal back

actually false?* — under his standing doctrine that an
impossibility claim is the cheapest gate there is: those
who state something cannot be done are hiding something,
or are lazy and arrogant, and every refusal must show its
coordinates or fall.

The Second Reckoning (Abroad IV) showed the coordinates.
Thirty-nine sources later, the refusal stood **FALSE as
literally worded and TRUE as evidently meant**: three
attested, published, fixed kana→number banks exist — and
every one of them is modern, commercial, and esoteric,
the earliest dated 2003, none belonging to any historical
Japanese tradition. The repair wording was adopted and
stands as this volume's front gate, verbatim:

> **No kana letter-value bank is attested before the 21st
> century, and none belongs to any historical Japanese
> tradition.**

Essay IV's Gate, in its own words: **DOES NOT FAIL** as
a second reckoning at its declared tiers — **FAILS**, at
the same size, in the same breath, as anything stronger:
as proof that no kana bank predates 2003 anywhere in
print, or that none was ever sealed, burned, or lost
unrecorded (n.1, n.2, n.3). The founder's suppression
hypothesis — something hidden *from* the Japanese, or

exception, and no exhibit places a gematria inside any
archive that died. The hypothesis stands as a live
suspicion with coordinates, neither crowned nor buried.

**What the gate governs, and what it does not.** The
narrowed refusal governs *attested transfer*: no value

A bank openly founded in 2026, under the Flagpole, dated

**G1 — The axis: gojūon positional, read as the dialect
reads its own table.** Values are sequence positions in
the gojūonzu grid, read row-major in the spoken column

お = 5, か = 6 … を = 47, ん = 48; the voiced block

bank is its reading order and nothing else; the only
order with a claim on a Japanese bank is the order the
dialect's own table is read in (n.4) — Abroad III's

another founder's deviation, his fingerprint; the mint
declines the inheritance and prints the coordinate: the
two readings of one grid differ by exactly one
transposition, the i- and o-columns swapping offsets,
the a-, u-, and e-columns holding the same within-row
offset in both banks. Neither reading is the other's
error. **The inventory:** 47 grid signs, with the や
row's *yi* and *ye* cells and the わ row's *wu* cell
standing as **holes** — no kana, no seat, printed as
vacancies; the grid holds their places and the bank
seats no ghosts. **ゐ (45) and ゑ (46) are seated as
ARCHIVAL seats** — the dialect's own digamma, struck
from the living table in 1946, held under the house's
disposition law: marked, never vanished. **を takes its
grid seat, 47**, the seat kept though its sound
surrendered to お. **ん takes the next seat after the
grid: 48 — ONE value.** Fukada's dual ン (1 or 10, by
visibility) is refused: a sign with two values is a fork

katakana are one sign in two dresses and share every

**G2 — The dakuten law: continuation, on the mint's own

**G3 — The chōon law: the mark weighs the vowel it
lengthens.** ー is **an operator, not a sign**: it weighs
the seat value of the vowel it lengthens — かー = カ 6 +
あ 1; とー = ト 20 + お 5; きー = キ 7 + い 2. A ー with
no vowel before it weighs 0. *Reasoning:* Abroad III
seats the long vowel as the dialect's own OFFSET — +1
mora, declared in the spelling — and the constant is one
more mora *of the same quality*: the lengthened vowel is
weighed twice because it is sounded twice. Yoshino's

**The declared seam, printed:** length spelled with a
vowel kana weighs as written — おう = 5 + 3 = **8**,
where the length-reading would give 10 (Δ2, printed);
えい = 4 + 2 = **6** as written. The mark ー has no
office but length, so its operator reading is
unambiguous; a vowel kana in second position is
sometimes length and sometimes a true second mora
（思う *omou* sounds its う whole), and a sign bank
cannot read morphology. The seam stands exhibited, not
hidden; a future ruling may close it, and the totals
