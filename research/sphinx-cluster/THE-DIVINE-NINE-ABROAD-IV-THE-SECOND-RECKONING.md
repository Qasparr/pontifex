## II. THE METHOD OF THE SECOND RECKONING

The method is the refusal made to show its coordinates. Abroad
III's refusal rested on three absences — no seam, no constant,
no mirror — stated for the Milesian transfer. The existence
wording that rode on it ("no attested tradition assigns the
kana number values"; "none exists to claim") is broader: it
denies a *class of object*. To test a denial of a class, the
audit first fixes the class, then walks every candidate the
record offers into the light, one at a time, and rules each at
its own coordinates. No candidate is convicted or acquitted by
the company it keeps.

**The class, fixed.** A *gematria bank* — the object whose
existence is in question — is a **fixed sign→value table** for
the signs of a script, **used to compute word totals**, with
**equal totals treated as meaningful** — the isopsephy move:
convert, sum, equate. Greek Milesian and Hebrew gematria are
the type cases [ATTESTED as systems]. Three neighbouring
objects are *not* the class, and the audit refuses to let them
impersonate it:

- **(a) Number-names** — a script spelling the words for
  numbers ("one, two, three") is not a value table; every
  literate language has these.
- **(b) Inverse wordplay** — reading a digit string *as* words
  runs number→sign, the opposite direction, and if the reading
  is chosen per case from a menu, there is no fixed table at
  all.
- **(c) Counts of something else** — strokes, dates, positions
  in a list: the numbers attach to the grapheme's shape or the
  calendar, not to the sign as a value.

**Attestation is not antiquity.** A system published in a
dated modern book is attested — *as a modern system*. Almost
everything in this audit turns on keeping those two apart,
because the record, as §III shows, contains both a real
attestation and a false antiquity, and they belong to different
objects: the banks are attested; their claimed age is not.

**The verdict words.** Each candidate is ruled in the research
dossier's three terms, which the Gate adopts: **FALSIFIES**
(the candidate is a member of the class whose existence the
wording denied), **PRESSES** (the candidate is adjacent —
inverse, partial, or the wrong species of bank), **DOES NOT

belongs to another question). A candidate may falsify the

**The founder's hypothesis, carried as a question.** §V tests

## IV. THE DATA II — THE CANDIDATES THAT DO NOT CARRY

### IV.1 — Classical kotodama-gaku: a bank of meanings, never of numbers

The early-modern and modern kotodama-*gaku* line — Yamaguchi
Shidō, Nakamura Kōdō, Ōishi Gisu, Honda Chikaatsu's reigaku,
Deguchi Onisaburō's Ōmoto genrei-gaku — assigns each of the
fifty (or seventy-five) sounds a **meaning, a deity, a
cosmogonic role**: five father-sounds, nine mother-sounds,
seventy-five sounds each with its kami. It is a sign→*meaning*
bank, complete and sophisticated — and it contains no numbers,
no summing, no equating of words by totals. [ATTESTED at the
research tier.] The word "kazutama" in Meiji seimei literature
meant likewise the fortune-meanings of *stroke totals* (§IV.4),
not kana values. **Verdict: DOES NOT TOUCH** — and it is the
audit's most instructive acquittal: Japan built an elaborate
science of the kana as *signs of meaning* for centuries without
once making them signs of number. The neighbouring art is not
the art.

### IV.2 — Goroawase: PRESSES, in the inverse direction

語呂合わせ — reading digit strings as words (794 = 鳴くよ,
*naku yo*, Heian-kyō; 1333 = 一味散々） — is attested from the
Edo Tenmei era (1781–89) in its wordplay sense and is
schoolbook-commonplace in its mnemonic sense [ATTESTED —
dictionary tier]. It proves a deep cultural affinity for
number↔sound conversion, and the audit does not minimise it.
But its direction is number→word; its readings are **chosen per
case** from a menu of three to five per digit (1 = ichi / hi /
hito / wan…), which is the precise anti-property of a bank; and
its strings are read, never summed — totals play no role
anywhere in it. **Verdict: PRESSES** — the affinity is real,
the bank is absent, and goroawase's freedom is itself evidence
of the absence: a culture with a fixed kana bank would not need
to shop for readings.

### IV.3 — Hifumi: number-names, never values

ひ・ふ・み・よ・い・む・な・や・こ・と are the clipped heads of
the native numerals *hitotsu, futatsu, mittsu, yottsu, itsutsu,
muttsu, nanatsu, yattsu, kokonotsu, tō* — the number-**names**
1–10 themselves, not values assigned to kana [ATTESTED — the
National Diet Library's reference record treats the sequence as
the counting substitute at the head of the 47-sound Futo-norito].
In norito recitation the chant continues to 100, 1000, 10,000 —
place-value number-names again. No source found — academic,
devotional, or commercial — applies hi = 1, fu = 2 to arbitrary
words to compute totals; the 47 sounds are *chanted*, their
power kotodama, not arithmetic. The antiquity coordinate is
printed whole: the wago numerals are genuinely ancient (Old
Japanese); the fixed 47-sound Futo-norito is not thereby
ancient, and the Hifumi Shinji corpus is Okamoto Tenmei's
automatic writing of **1944–1961**. (It is Yoshino's bank,
§III.2, that borrows the hifumi sequence as its ordering —
a modern bank standing on an ancient counting-song, each at
its own date.) **Verdict: DOES NOT TOUCH** — object (a) of
§II: names, not values.

### IV.4 — Seimei handan: strokes, not sign-values

姓名判断， formalised by Kumazaki Ken'ō (1881–1961; system
published from 1918, 『姓名の神秘』 1929), derives its five
grids — ten-kaku, jin-kaku, chi-kaku, gai-kaku, sō-kaku —
**purely from kanji stroke counts**, each grid read against
fixed fortune-meanings for 1–81, wrapping modulo 81 [ATTESTED;
the 1–81 "spirit-number" table was Kumazaki's own construction
on I Ching number theory, per the 2021 scholarly re-reading].
The kana variant exists and confirms the rule: services
accepting kana names count **the kana's own stroke counts**
（あ = 3, ア = 2, schools differing) — strokes again, never
intrinsic sign-values; a secondary sound-technique reads
five-elements correspondences, not numbers. **Verdict: DOES
NOT TOUCH** — object (c) of §II: it counts something else.
Its numbers would attach identically to any script's shapes;
they are a fortune table for totals, not a value table for
signs.

### IV.5 — Katakamuna and the jindai moji: the forgery consensus, and no values

The Katakamuna documents surface in **1949**, when Narasaki
Kōgetsu claimed to have copied a shrine scroll on Mt. Rokko;
**no original exists or has ever been exhibited**, and the
corpus is classed by scholarship as *gisho* — forgery, of the
ko-shi-koden stratum [ATTESTED as the scholarly consensus].
Narasaki's own reading of the eighty Utahi assigns the sounds
meanings, not numbers; the numeric bank built on the Utahi
order is Yoshino's, dated 2012+ and already counted at §III.2
— it may not be counted twice, once as modern bank and once

asks: **no jindai moji tradition assigns its signs numeric

**Verdict: DOES NOT TOUCH** as an antiquity claim; PRESSES

### IV.6 — The Siddham channel: the road was open; this cargo is not in it

Siddham reached Japan with Kūkai's return in **806** and was
preserved in Shingon as bonji — seed syllables, each tied to a
deity: a sign→*deity/sound* correspondence, not a sign→number
one [ATTESTED]. What demonstrably *did* travel is the Indic
phonetic **order**: the gojūon grid's arrangement is thought to
derive from Sanskrit śikṣā via Siddham and Chinese fanqie, the
earliest gojūon-style layout in the *Kujakukyō Ongi*
(c. 1004–28). The genuine Indic gematria-analogue, **kaṭapayādi**
— consonants denoting digits, digits read in reverse — is, per
Sarma's academic history (2012), essentially a **Kerala**
system, rarely used even in north India; no source — academic,
Shingon, or shittan-gaku — shows kaṭapayādi or bhūtasaṅkhyā
applied to kana or bonji in Japan, or present in the Siddham
curriculum transmitted via China. **Verdict: DOES NOT TOUCH** —
with its shape noted, because the shape matters to §V: the
absence here is a *positive datum*, not a mere gap. The channel
was open and used for a thousand years; the letter-order
crossed on it; the letter-number systems did not — so far as
any source shows. A hidden crossing would have had to use the
one road whose traffic is documented, and leave no mark on it.

### IV.7 — The Meiji ciphers: real banks, wrong species

The Foreign Ministry's first kana substitution table dates to
**1874**; the **1904 kana code** （仮名符号） assigned each kana
a fixed two-digit number, in two alternating tables （以号/呂号，
rotated by month) — and the values were **rotated for secrecy**
(in 1905, add 1 to every code), which is the anti-gematria
property stated as procedure: a bank whose values must change
is a cipher, and no cipher source sums code values or equates
words by totals [ATTESTED — JACAR records via the Cryptiana

encoded kana by gojūon coordinates. **Verdict: PRESSES as
banks** — fixed sign↔number tables indisputably existed in
Meiji Japan — **DOES NOT TOUCH as gematria**: the species is
cryptography, and its totals mean nothing to anyone, by design.

**What it cannot establish:** anything about a letter-number

### V.2 — Haibutsu kishaku and the Shugendō ban: the mechanism at scale [A]

From **1868**, under the Kami–Buddha Separation Order, the
haibutsu kishaku movement destroyed temples, images, and
**texts**, and forcibly laicised monks nationwide; the
literature's magnitudes run to a pessimistic estimate of some
40,000 temples destroyed, and 18,000 eradicated in 1872–74
alone [A at the cited-estimate tier]. Esoteric archives —
Shingon, Tendai, Shugendō — died wholesale in it. The **1872**
Shugendō abolition (Dajōkan Proclamation No. 273, 15 September
1872) folded the Honzan, Tōzan, and Haguro lines into Tendai
and Shingon and scattered their practitioners; transmission was
interrupted and subordinated for some seventy years — and
**not annihilated**: independent Shugendō bodies re-formed
immediately after 1945, from surviving lineages and texts.

**The limit, printed plainly:** no source — scholarly or
apologetic — names any letter-number lore, kana table, or
gematria-like text among these losses. The coordinate proves a
mechanism that *could* have killed such a tradition; it is not
evidence that one existed to be killed. And the counterweight
stands in the same record: mikkyō survived these very events
institutionally (the Kōyasan and Tōji archives intact), and
Shugendō's carriers endured to re-emerge. "Esoteric" in Meiji
Japan did not mean "automatically erased."

### V.3 — The survival record: the hypothesis weighed on what sealed things do

Against the losses, the ledger enters what suppression and
sealing demonstrably *failed* to do in Japan, because the
hypothesis must be weighed on the whole record:

- **The Taiseikyō bonfire (1679–81) [A].** The *Sendai Kuji
  Hongi Taiseikyō*, printed 1679, was banned by the bakufu
  within about two years on Ise protests; **its printing
  blocks were burnt** and its editor placed under house
  arrest — and the National Institute of Japanese Literature
  counts **over 100 extant copies**. Deliberate, successful
  suppression of a Shinto text in early-modern Japan failed to
  erase it.
- **The Shintō Gobusho （神道五部書） [A].** Five Watarai texts
  of the 13th century with falsely Nara-era colophons —
  described in the standard dictionary as secret works
  **restricted to members of the Watarai family aged over
  sixty**: the founder's second hiding direction in its best
  historical dress, a genuinely lineage-sealed, age-gated
  corpus. It **survives in full**, has been read by scholars
  for three centuries since Yoshimi Yukikazu exposed it in
  1736, and contains enshrinement apologetics and theology —
  **no kana-number material**.
- **The kanjō corpora [A].** Medieval transmission ran through
  initiation (Rambelli, 2002): from late Heian and Kamakura,
  kanjō became secret initiation — kuden, hiden — and the
  kami-focused shintō kanjō framed its matter by the Reikiki;
  the Reiki kanjō is first recorded in **1350** at Shinpukuji,
  and its apparatus — texts, scrolls, certificates, kirigami —
  **survives and is studied**. Yoshida Shintō graded its
  secrecy into eight stages, its kirigami memorised and
  returned — a genuine near-paperless mechanism. And across
  every academic summary of what those channels carried, the
  contents are: myth exegesis, yin–yang union symbolism,
  regalia and mandala diagrams, ritual procedure, deity
  identifications. **Never, in any surfaced corpus, a
  letter-number bank.**
- **The Nakatomi Harae line [A].** A commentary tradition held
  "most secret" at Ise's Sengū-in, copied in 1369, surviving
  (a 1607 copy by Bonshun among them) — and the only numerals
  in the surviving copy are the **section numbers** of a
  liturgy's division: structural numbering, object (c) of §II,
  not values of kana.
- **The one true scarcity [A].** The National Institute of
  Japanese Literature records that shintō kanjō was hardly
  performed after Meiji and that **complete surviving sets of
  its materials are few**. This is the ledger's strongest
  coordinate for the founder: a sealed-transmission *channel*
  genuinely died in Meiji. Its scope, printed with it: the
  statement concerns kami-initiations in general — it attests
  a broken pipe, not what, beyond the documented exegesis,
  ever flowed through it.

**The ledger's balance.** Japanese sealed traditions leaked,
were graded, copied, returned, catalogued, banned, burnt — and
**surfaced**. A kana gematria practised inside that culture
would be the sole major sealed system to leave **zero**
residue in an unusually well-surfaced record: no table in any
opened corpus, no trace in the ciphers that borrowed the
syllabary, no echo in the meaning-bank of kotodama-gaku that
catalogued every sound's deity while numbering none. Possible —
the boundary of §I is not moved — but it is the hypothesis
that needs the evidence, and at every coordinate where
evidence could stand, the record shows other contents.
