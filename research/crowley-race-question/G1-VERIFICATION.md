# G-1 VERIFICATION — Old Comment III:75, עם vs 111

*Subagent verification, 2026-10-11. Findings, never doctrine.*

## The question

The sweep's G-1: Old Comment III:75 claims "עם = 111 … 111 × 6 = 666"
and "עם with ם final adds to 671," but עם = 70+40 = 110 (670). Small
exact +1, systematic twice, load-bearing on the 666 equation. Transcription
slip or Crowley's?

## Work log

- **Hypothesis:** the 1912 first publication (Equinox I:7, "LIBER LEGIS; THE
  COMMENT," pp. 387–400a) would settle it — published in Crowley's lifetime
  under his direction.
- **Method:** located the IA item `oto-equinox-vol-1-no-01-10`
  ("Equinox Vol 1 No 07.pdf"), downloaded, found the III:75 comment on PDF
  p. 439 (= printed p. 400a), rendered at 300 dpi, and read the Hebrew
  glyphs visually at 4–6× zoom — no OCR reliance for the letters.
- **Observation:** the printed Hebrew word is **אעם** (aleph-ayin-mem),
  three letters, not the two-letter עם of both web transcriptions. The
  rightmost glyph matches the aleph in the adjacent "הא = 6" exactly;
  the middle glyph is ayin; the leftmost is a closed final mem.
- **Dead end:** two crop attempts missed the line before landing it —
  noted so the next verifier aims at PDF p. 439 directly.
- **Result:** אעם = 1+70+40 = **111** ✓. And "with a ם final" =
  א(1)+ע(70)+ם(600, mispar gadol) = **671** ✓ — the text explicitly flags
  the final-mem valuation. 111×6=666 ✓. Every number on the page is
  arithmetically exact.

## Conclusion

**(a) What the source shows:** the 1912 print reads אעם = 111 and
אעם (final-mem valued) = 671. Both correct. There is no +1 discrepancy
in Crowley's text.

**(b) Source verified:** *The Equinox*, Vol. I No. 7 (London: Wieland &
Co., March 1912), "LIBER LEGIS; THE COMMENT," printed p. 400a —
Internet Archive item `oto-equinox-vol-1-no-01-10`
(https://archive.org/download/oto-equinox-vol-1-no-01-10/Equinox%20Vol%201%20No%2007.pdf),
PDF p. 439, glyphs read visually at zoom. (1912 = public domain, US.)

**(c) Assessment:** **G-1 is dead.** Both web transcriptions
(tarrdaniel.com, otohungary.org) dropped the small initial aleph,
reading אעם as עם — a transcription artifact, exactly like the Yophiel
dissolution. What Crowley published is arithmetically coherent:
111, 671 (via flagged mispar gadol), and the load-bearing 666 all
verify. Neither error nor breadcrumb — the anomaly never existed outside
the transcriptions. (The kolel hypothesis is now unnecessary: the
numbers work without it.)

**(d) Not verified:** Crowley's holograph manuscript of the Old Comment
was not consulted — but the 1912 print was set under his direction and
is internally self-consistent (the 666 equation *requires* the aleph),
so a manuscript check could not resurrect the anomaly.
