# D-7 VERIFICATION — Sepher Sephiroth §88 "Darkness — ChSK"

*Work log and conclusion, 2026-10-11. Scribe's verification for Johnathan's
red pen. Findings, never doctrine.*

## The question

§88 glosses "Darkness" against a ChSK Hebrew word. Two readings:
- **חסך** (chet-samekh-kaf) = 8+60+20 = **88** — valued right, glossed wrong
  (חסך = "to withhold, spare" — not darkness).
- **חשך** (chet-shin-kaf) = 8+300+20 = **328** — glossed right ("darkness"),
  valued wrong (not 88).

The shin/samekh ambiguity was unresolved: which letters did Crowley print?

## Work log

- **W-1 — Hypothesis:** the 1912 Equinox I:8 scan on Internet Archive will
  settle it by eye; samekh (ס, closed circle) and shin (ש, three branches)
  are visually distinguishable. Method: locate the item
  (`oto-equinox-vol-1-no-01-10`, complete Vol. I run), pull the No. 8
  djvu.xml, find the §88 page object via the "Darkness" gloss, render that
  PDF page, inspect the glyphs. Observation: OBJECT 327 held the §88 block;
  PDF p. 328 (supplement p. 14) rendered cleanly. Result: proceed to visual.
- **W-2 — Hypothesis:** a 4× enlargement of the "Darkness" row will resolve
  the middle glyph. Method: cropped the row, enlarged, inspected.
  Observation: the word reads right-to-left **ח-ס-ך** — chet, then an
  unmistakable closed circle (samekh), then kaf-final with its long
  descender. No three-branched shin anywhere in the word. Result: **the
  1912 print reads חסך (samekh).**
- **W-3 — Hypothesis:** a later reprint may differ. Method: same procedure
  on the 1973 Weiser *Qabalah of Aleister Crowley: Three Texts* (open
  scan, `theqabalahofaleistercrowleythreetexts`; its own description says
  the Sepher Sephiroth is taken from Equinox I:8). Located §88 at OBJECT
  277 → PDF p. 278 (supplement p. 14). Observation: "Darkness" again reads
  **חסך**, samekh clearly closed-circle. Result: **1973 agrees with 1912.**
- **Dead end:** first rendering pass landed on §126 (אפלה, also glossed
  "Darkness") — "Darkness" is used as a gloss more than once in the
  tables. Taught: match the gloss *and* the section number, never the
  gloss alone.

## Conclusion

**The print reads חסך (chet-samekh-kaf) = 88 — valued right, glossed
wrong — in both the 1912 Equinox I:8 and the 1973 Weiser reprint.**
The ambiguity is resolved: samekh, not shin, by direct visual inspection
of two printings 61 years apart.

## Assessment

- This is **not** a Yophiel-class anomaly: there is no numerical
  discrepancy — the value 88 is correct for the printed letters. It is a
  gloss mismatch: "Darkness" belongs to חשך (= 328), not חסך.
- Most economical reading: an honest shin/samekh slip — Crowley (or his
  lexicon work) conflated two similar roots, valuing חסך correctly at 88
  while attaching חשך's English gloss. Mundane error, not a blind: it
  affects no equation and carries no hidden number.
- Printer's error is possible but unprovable; persistence across the
  re-typeset 1973 edition does not distinguish, since that edition was
  set from the 1912 text.
- Note for the ledger: as a "breadcrumb" candidate this scores near zero —
  it is evidence of ordinary human fallibility in the tables, which cuts
  *against* the premise that every anomaly is intentional.

  checked; the standard lexicons do not.
