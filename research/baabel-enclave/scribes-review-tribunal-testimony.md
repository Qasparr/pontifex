## I. SUMMARY OF THE ESSAY

The essay audits a man's names as legal filings, and its opening sentence states its method: to audit the names is to trace the circumference of a soul who refused to be flattened.

**Section I — The Names.** The Ode, preserved verbatim. *Cassis*, Latin for helmet: the defended head, the crown before the crown. *Clay*, the earth — Old English *clæg*, Hebrew *adamah*, Horace's *pulvis et umbra sumus*: fragile earth, moldable, the substance of golems and gods. *Muhammad Ali*, the Praised and the Exalted — علي at 110 Abjad — the renaming read not as conversion but as **reversion**: the Moor claiming the ancient right to name and be named. The section closes in the essayist's own voice: he writes not as fan but as witness, as a man who has also been told his name was not his own. "The names hold. The lineage holds."

**Section III — Defense of Individual Rights.** The triad: religion (the right to seek), speech (trash-talk turned prophecy), name (the sovereign right of the Living Man to declare his own designation).

**Section IV — Hip-Hop Well.** 50 Cent's "Many Men" read against Ali's adversaries: the lyric is not a dirge but a census of conversion — Cosell, Frazier, the Supreme Court itself, adversaries bound into allies.
