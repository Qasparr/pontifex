# COMMON-LINK — what the twelve banks share, and what they don't

*Synthesis of the twelve-tongues sweep. Draft for the author's red pen —
findings, never doctrine.*

## The question

Twelve workers, one corpus, twelve tongues: where do the languages meet,
and is there a common link — a number, a structure, a reason — running
through the meetings?

## The answer in brief

Three numbers meet across banks — **77, 93, 418** — and 32 stands as a
single-bank calibration. The meetings are carried by exactly two
mechanisms, and neither is mysterious:

1. **Shared inheritance.** The Phoenician 22-order runs through Hebrew,
   Aramaic, Arabic, Greek, and Coptic numerals. Where the same cognate
   (עז/عز/οζ/ⲟⲍ) is weighed, 70=70 and 7=7 everywhere — one inheritance
   in four scripts, not four independent witnesses.
2. **Native spellings on native tables.** THELEMA=AGAPE=93 works because
   θέλημα and ἀγάπη are Greek words on the Greek table. AXONEME=77 works
   because AXONEME is spelled natively in the Latin alphabet on the
   ordinal table.

There is **no number in this corpus that arises independently in three
or more unrelated tables for the same concept** — no meeting that cannot
be accounted for by inheritance or native spelling. That is itself the
common link: **the bridge is a property of the (word, table) pair, not
of the number alone.** The Latin worker proved it in miniature:
AXONEME=77 in ordinal, 431 in Agrippa — same word, same script, no
bridge, because the table changed.

## The three meetings, ranked

### 1. 77 — the strongest candidate (independent banks: 3, one inherited)

AXONEME=77 (Latin ordinal, native English spelling, ATTESTED) ↔ עז=77
(Hebrew, native, ATTESTED). This is the only meeting in the sweep where
two genuinely different tables meet on two native spellings — and it is
the author's canonical example, reproduced, not discovered. Arabic's
عز=77 is an independent table sharing the inheritance; Greek and Coptic
are echoes of it.

### 2. 418 — the most convention-sensitive (independent banks: 2, inherited)

ABRAHADABRA=418 lands unforced in Hebrew under the mechanical
transliteration and reproduces in Arabic — but every 418 in the sweep
depends on transliteration choices (H→ه vs H→ح moves Arabic 418→421;
the Hebrew HERU-RA-HA fork is 423 vs the established 418). The
Semitic abjad inheritance carries the Hebrew↔Arabic agreement
letter-for-letter. It is a candidate, and the most fragile one.

### 3. 93 — a Greek-bank phenomenon (independent banks: 1)

THELEMA=AGAPE=93, authentic spellings, attested table — the bank's
strongest convergence and a faithful reproduction of the classic. It
crosses nowhere else. Coptic's 93 is descent, not corroboration.

## Where bridges cluster — and where they fail

**They cluster** on shared ancestry (the 22-order → 77 across four
scripts) and on words at home in their tables (93 in Greek).

**They fail** everywhere a word travels: AXONEME's 77 does not cross to
Semitic spelling (267); THELEMA's 93 does not cross to Hebrew (481);
CELL's 32 does not cross to Greek (85) or Armenian (125); ABRAHADABRA's
418 does not cross to Greek (221) and fails closed in Coptic (ϩ has no
value). **Each word weighed in its own tongue predicts exactly this
non-reproduction** — the doctrine's own negative control, and it held.

**The independence proofs came from failure:** Armenian shares the
ciphered-additive *principle* but not the abjad *order* — and reproduces
nothing (zero target hits). That is what an actually-independent bank
looks like when there is no bridge: silence. By contrast the banks that
reproduce are exactly the banks that inherit.

## The anti-findings (load-bearing)

- **I-1:** English ≡ Latin ordinal — one bank, not two. Any chart
  showing an "English↔Latin bridge" double-counts.
- **I-2:** Hebrew ≡ Aramaic — all eleven values identical. One bank.
- **D-1:** Coptic descends from Greek Milesian. Agreement is continuity.
- **D-2:** עז=77 across Hebrew/Arabic/Greek/Coptic is one Phoenician
  inheritance in four scripts.
- **Agrippa** dissolves the canonical bridge (77→431): the table, not
  the word, is load-bearing.
- **Sanskrit:** Katapayadi is an attested *encoding* system, not a
  word-sum — category verdict, not a bank.
- **Japanese:** no native letter-number tradition (all claims NEGATIVE
  against sources). **Chinese:** 姓名学 is a luck chart, not a table;
  character choice is arbitrary on three axes. **Ge'ez liturgical:**
  no letter values — but the Debtera halehame enumeration is a genuine
  folk-magical practice, the sweep's one positive surprise.

## Collisions worth flagging

- Arabic HERU-RA-HA = ABRAHADABRA = 418: the two 418-terms collide
  in-bank. Whether that is signal or the abjad's small value-space is
  red-pen business.
- Greek THELEMA = AGAPE = 93: the classic equation, reproduced.
- TRVVTH hits no target in any of the seven banks that weighed it
  (110 / 1012 / 1209 / 413 / 1106 / 15009 / 688). The framework's own
  doctrinal word is numerically silent everywhere — filed without
  interpretation.

## Chance accounting

~200 weighings × 6 targets across the sweep. METHOD.md §5 governs:
coincidences are guaranteed. LIBERTY=11 (Chinese stroke-count,
CONSTRUCTED, scribe-chosen characters) is filed as chance, explicitly.
The 418s and the 77 survive chance-accounting better — they reproduce
across banks under stated conventions — but "reproduces under stated
conventions" is not "true": every reproduction here names its
convention, and the conventions are the author's to rule on.

## The honest limit

This sweep weighed eleven Latin-script terms (plus עז) outward into
twelve tongues. It did not weigh native words *of* those tongues
against each other — the design finds bridges *from* the author's
corpus, not bridges *between* foreign words. A reverse sweep (native
terms per tongue, weighed natively, then compared) is the obvious next
instrument, and it is unbuilt.
