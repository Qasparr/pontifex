## R1 — The HERU-RA-HA 423/418 fork: is e-elision a standing rule or a one-off?

**What the books say:**
- Mechanical transliteration (every letter rendered): HERU-RA-HA → ההרוראהא = **423** (`hebrew.md` §2, l.44).
- The established spelling: הרו־רא־הא = **418**. The 5-point gap is *exactly* one ה — the E of HERU, elided (HERU→הרו, not ההרו). The merger ה:[e,h] "fired with loss" on HERU-RA-HA (`hebrew.md` l.26, l.72).
- Hand arithmetic and `weigh_hebrew.py` agreed independently (423), so the gap is real, not a script artifact (`hebrew.md` l.104).

**What turns on it:**
- If e-elision stands as a rule: the HERU-RA-HA 418 leg of M3 holds, and the rule must be stated generally (it also fired in THELEMA).
- If it is struck as a one-off: the HERU-RA-HA 418 leg falls to 423. M3 does **not** fully collapse — ABRAHADABRA=418 in Hebrew is mechanical and unforced (`CORRESPONDENCE.md` M3) — but the 418 *bridge between the two terms* loses its Hebrew leg.

## R2 — The Arabic 418s: H→ه or H→ح?

**What the books say:**
- H→ه: HERU-RA-HA = **418**, ABRAHADABRA = **418** — both hit the target; the in-bank collision (C1) is 418/418 (`arabic.md` l.59–60).
- H→ح: both move to **421** — the target hit is gone, but the collision *survives* (421/421) (`arabic.md` l.72–73, l.82–87).
- The sweep's own note: ح is "Egyptologically the better reading for 'Heru'" (`arabic.md` l.171–172).

**What turns on it:**
- Ruling ه keeps the 418 target hit on Arabic evidence — and with it the claim that the 418 reproduces across Hebrew and Arabic banks.
- Ruling ح trades the target hit for the Egyptologically better reading; the two terms still collide in-bank at 421, so the Arabic bank keeps a convergence, just not a target one.
