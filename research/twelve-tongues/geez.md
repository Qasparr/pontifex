## 1. The verdict (first, because it decides everything)

The expected negative finding did **not** hold cleanly. The investigation
found two separate things, and they must not be confused:

1. **The Ethiopic numerals (፩ ፪ ፫ … ፲ ፳ ፻ ፼) are NOT letter values.**
   They are a numeral-glyph system for *writing numbers* — borrowed from the
   Greek numerals, possibly via Coptic uncials, historically written as Greek
   letters over- and under-lined with a vinculum. This is the Roman-numerals
   comparison from the task brief, and it is confirmed. **Negative on this
   point: Ge'ez fidel characters carry no abjad-style values in any living
   mainstream tradition.**

2. **But a genuine native letter-number practice exists anyway** — the
   **Debtera (halehame) enumeration**: the first-form fidel characters in
   the halehame order carry values ሀ=1, ለ=2, ሐ=3 … ተ=10, ኀ=20 … ፐ=800,
   used by the *debtera* (unordained clergy who double as astrologers,
   herbalists, scribes, and fortune-tellers) for fortune-telling — e.g. a
   person's mother's name summed, mod 12, read against the monthly star.
   It is a specialized magical practice, not liturgical mainstream — and it
   is the tradition's own table, not the scribe's invention.

So the bank is weighed three ways: (a) the Debtera enumeration (table
ATTESTED, word-forms CONSTRUCTED transliterations); (b) the repo's
Hebrew-fallback doctrine (values ATTESTED, map CONSTRUCTED); (c) a
transparent scribe-built fidel ordinal (CONSTRUCTED, labeled as such).

## 2. The investigation — sources and what each one establishes

### 2a. The numerals are glyphs, not letter values

- **Wikipedia, "Geʽez script"** (http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geʽez_script):
  "Geʽez uses an additional alphabetic numeral system comparable to the
  Hebrew, Arabic abjad and Greek numerals… The digits historically are
  Greek letters over- and under-lined with a vinculum." And: "Ethiopian
  numerals were borrowed from the Greek numerals, possibly via Coptic
  uncial letters." The numeral table (፩=1 … ፺=90, ፻=100, ፼=10,000)
  maps 1:1 against Greek Α Β Γ … Ρ, not against fidel letters. What this
  establishes: the ፩-style glyphs are a *number-writing* system of Greek
  descent. Nothing in the article assigns numeric values to the fidel
  characters themselves.
- **"A Look at Ethiopic Numerals"** (https://dejeniea.com/A_Look_at_Ethiopic_Numerals.html,
  Daniel Yacob's Ethiopian computing site): confirms the Greek/Coptic
  derivation, notes the archaic-letter insertions for 6 and 90, and the
  "Omicron-Ayne axis" at 70 — then explicitly separates this from
  "numerology tables (another subject!)". What this establishes: even the
  people who built the Unicode Ethiopic block treat the *numerals* and the
  *letter-number practice* as two different subjects.

### 2b. The Debtera enumeration — a genuine native practice

- **geez.org, "Numerology"** (http://www.geez.org/Numerals/Numerology.html):
  the key source. It documents two enumerations. First, the "Gematria
  Enumeration" (first-form fidel arranged in the *Hebrew* order, locked to
  Hebrew/Greek values 1–400, extended to 900 and beyond) — but its own
  author writes that this arrangement "appears to be an attempt to
  superimpose the Gematria discipline of Hebrew and Greek mysticism onto
  the Ge'ez character set" and that its applicability "is not well
  understood; it may be a scholarly alternative to the preferred
  'Halehame' system of the debtere." **The Abegede ordering is therefore
  a scholarly reconstruction — CONSTRUCTED — and is NOT used as a bank
  in this survey** (it would in any case be the Hebrew table in Ge'ez
  dress, redundant with the Hebrew bank).
  
  Second, the **"Debtera (Halehame) Enumeration"**: 26 first-form orders in
  the halehame sequence with values 1–800, the seven vowel forms acting as
  multipliers (×1…×7), with the explicit note that "example usage of the
  system to find the numeric value of words invariably uses the letters of
  a word in the first form only." The page gives a worked example —
  ክርስቶስ (Christos) = ከ50 + ረ6 + ሰ7 + ቶ10 + ሰ7 = **80** — which this
  survey uses as its calibration check. It also gives the fortune-telling
  use (mother's name → sum → mod 12 → monthly star), noting the practice
  "while still in use plays a role in modern society analogous to a
  'Zodiac' type fortune telling system." The page's own lineage note:
  "it is probable that Copts, Greeks and Ethiopians were making an effort
  to synchronize the numeric values assigned to letters with the
  equivalent letters and values used in the Hebrew 'Gematria' practice.
  Thus transporting the practice to a limited extent." — i.e. the practice
  may itself be downstream of Hebrew gematria contact. **Stated, not
  hidden; tiered ATTESTED as the tradition's own table, with the lineage
  caveat on the record.**
- **H-Net review of Ayele Bekerie, *Ethiopic, An African Writing System:
  Its History and Principles*** (https://rmtrain.h-net.org/reviews/showpdf.php?id=2854,
  reviewer Peter T. Daniels — the writing-systems scholar): "AB describes
  an equivalent to Hebrew gematria, where a numerical value is assigned to
  each character… each simple letter (consonant plus a) represents a
  number (h = 1, l = 2, …, p = 800) and the vowel variations represent
  multipliers (hu = 2, lu = 4, …, po = 5600); but in all the examples of
  numerological calculations, the vowel variations are not taken into
  account." What this establishes: the same h=1…p=800 system, in a
  published dissertation/book (1994/1997), independently of geez.org —
  and the same "first-form-only in practice" observation. Two independent
  attestations of one table.
- **ScienceBlog on Bekerie's book** (https://www3.scienceblog.com/community/older/archives/I/corn1444.html):
  Bekerie's contention that Ethiopic "goes beyond language; it also
  encompasses properties such as philosophy, numerology, ideography and
  astronomy" — "a knowledge system." Context only; it establishes that a
  numerology dimension of the script has at least one scholarly defender,
  not that the debtera table is liturgically mainstream.

### 2c. Fringe claims — documented, not used

- **greengoldgardens.com PDF** ("Geez-Language-001"): claims an "ancient
  and original order, called Abughida," assigning አ=1, በ=2, ገ=3, ደ=4,
  ሀ=5 … in the northern-Semitic ʼ–b–g–d order, tied to Psalm 118's 22
  letters and the "22 major arcana." This is modern web esotericism with no
  scholarly provenance — its "22 arcana" framing is the tell. Recorded so
  the red pen knows it was seen and rejected; its values are not used.

### 2d. Net verdict

- The numerals are not letter values: **negative, confirmed.**
- The Debtera halehame enumeration is a genuine, still-practiced,
  specialized Ethiopian letter-number tradition: **positive, with caveats**
  (folk-magical register, not liturgical; possible Hebrew-gematria
  influence "transported to a limited extent"; 26 orders only — ሸ ቸ ኘ ኸ
  ዠ and the labialized series have no value in it).
- The Abegede/Hebrew-order overlay is a scholarly reconstruction:
  **CONSTRUCTED, not used.**

### 3a. The transliteration convention (CONSTRUCTED — record it or it is nothing)

"Scribe phonetic key v1": vowels ride the አ-order (a→አ e→ኤ i→ኢ o→ኦ
u→ኡ y→ይ); consonants phonetic (b→በ d→ደ f→ፈ g→ገ h→ሀ j→ጀ k→ከ l→ለ
m→መ n→ነ p→ፐ q→ቀ r→ረ s→ሰ t→ተ v→ቨ w→ወ z→ዘ); θ ("th") → ተ — Ethiopic
has no θ; x → ከሰ (documented expansion, no single fidel for /ks/); c is
phonetic (/k/→ከ, /s/→ሰ — CELL read aloud as "sell"); greedy digraphs
ch→ቸ sh→ሸ th→ተ; hyphens dropped (HERU-RA-HA → ሀኤረኡረአሀአ).

Consequences the convention forces (all recorded per-row in the CSV):
- The greedy **th** digraph eats the final "th" of **TRVVTH** and the
  opening "th" of **THELEMA** — t+h are not weighed separately.
- The five አ-order vowel-forms (አ ኤ ኢ ኦ ኡ) all resolve to the አ-order
  base; **ይ resolves to የ-order** (90/23), not አ-order — the one that
  nearly slipped through (see §5, dead ends).
- ቨ (modern v-series) resolves to በ-order's base — a judgment call,
  documented.

## 4. Limits — what this survey does and does not claim

1. The Debtera enumeration is attested as a *folk-magical* practice of a
   specific class (the debtera), not as the letter-values of the Ge'ez
   language in general. Weighing Thelemic English terms in it is the
   scribe's application, not the tradition's.
2. The word-forms are transliterations of non-Ge'ez words by a
   scribe-built key. A native speaker's spelling choices (especially for
   TRVVTH's final θ and AXONEME's x) could differ; the key is on record
   so the red pen can re-run it.
3. The Abegede/Hebrew-order overlay was deliberately excluded as a
   scholarly reconstruction; the greengoldgardens "Abughida 22" as
   fringe. Both exclusions are appealable to the red pen.
4. The fidel ordinal is a construction — it is offered only so the
   survey's honest-fallback half is complete, and it is labeled in every
   row of the CSV.
5. One calibration: the script self-checks the Debtera table against the
   tradition's own worked example (ክርስቶስ = 80) before weighing anything;
   the run printed CALIBRATION OK.

finding holds") was therefore only half-triggered — the honest move was
to weigh the native bank *and* still do the fallback work, all labeled.

**Step 2 — dead end: the Abegede overlay.** H: the geez.org "Gematria
Enumeration" (fidel in Hebrew order = Hebrew values) could serve as the

R: rejected as scholarly reconstruction; recorded as CONSTRUCTED, not
used. (It would also be redundant — it *is* the Hebrew table.)

**Step 3 — dead end: the greengoldgardens "Abughida 22."** H: a 22-letter
Ge'ez gematria might be a folk tradition. M: read the snippet. O: the

**Step 4 — building the transliteration key.** H: a uniform phonetic key

**Step 6 — calibration.** H: if my Debtera transcription is right, the
