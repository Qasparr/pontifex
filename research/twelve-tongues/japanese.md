**Japanese has no native letter-number weighing tradition** — no table
mapping script units to numbers and applied to words, comparable to

## 1. AUDIT — the native-tradition claims

Standing audit flag (his): claims around Yijing/Ekikyō, onmyōdō, seimei
handan (姓名判断), and stroke-count divination are UNAUDITED. Audited
below, each against sources, verdict stated plainly.

### 1a. Seimei handan (姓名判断) — NEGATIVE (closest structural cousin, still not a weighing tradition)

**What it is.** A name-fortune system: count the brush strokes of each
kanji in a person's name, combine them into five counts — 天格 tenkaku
(surname total), 人格 jinkaku (last surname char + first given-name char),
地格 chikaku (given-name total), 外格 gaikaku (outer count), 総格 soukaku
(full-name total) — and rank each count against auspicious/inauspicious
numbers (大吉 daikichi numbers include 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11, 13, 15, 16,
21, 23, 24, 31, 32, 33, 41, 45; 大凶 daikyou include 2, 4, 9, 10, 12, 14,
19, 20, 22, …). Source: japanese-names.info explainer
(https://japanese-names.info/articles/seimei-handan-fortunetelling-based-on-your-name/).

**Why it fails the weighing test, four ways:**
1. **Names only.** It is applied to personal names, never to arbitrary
   words. There is no seimei-handan of "truth" or "liberty."
2. **No cross-word equivalence.** It never says word A = word B by shared
   number; it ranks single numbers as lucky/unlucky. Bridge-finding is not
   its operation.
3. **Not unified.** Multiple schools disagree on the counts themselves —
   e.g., whether to use 旧字体 or 新字体 stroke counts (the article's
   worked example: 郎 counted 9, some schools 14 via 郞).
4. **Modern.** The dominant Kumasaki-shiki (熊崎式) five-count framework was
   systematized by Kumasaki Kenoo in 1929 — Showa-era, not an ancient
   letter table. The article traces its roots through Chinese numerology
   and onmyōdō, but the system as used is a 20th-century construction.

Kana names *can* be analyzed (by kana stroke counts), but the system is
designed for kanji names. Verdict: a stroke-count fortune-telling of
names, **not** a letter-number weighing of words. NEGATIVE.

### 1b. Onmyōdō (陰陽道) — NEGATIVE

**What it is.** The Way of Yin and Yang: a court cosmology built on the
Chinese yin-yang and five-elements (五行: wood, fire, earth, metal, water)
philosophies, introduced to Japan in the 6th century, institutionalized
under the Taihō Code (701) as the Onmyōryō bureau with onmyōji officers
handling calendar-making, astronomical observation, and divination of
dates, times, directions, and general affairs. Abe no Seimei (921–1005)
is its famous Heian practitioner; it was prohibited as superstition in
the mid-19th century. Source: Wikipedia, "Onmyōdō"
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onmyōdō).

**Why it fails.** Numbers appear *cosmologically* — yin/yang as 2, the
five phases as 5, the gobosei pentagram diagramming the five elements'
overcoming cycle — but **no script unit is ever assigned a number** and
no word is ever weighed. There is no table; there is a cosmology. The
rikuban (divination board) computes positions of spirits relative to the
user — positional, not lexical. NEGATIVE.

### 1c. Ekikyō / Yijing number symbolism (Hetu 河圖, Luoshu 洛書) — NEGATIVE

**What it is.** The River Map and Luo Writing: cosmological number
diagrams. The Luoshu is a 3×3 magic square (every line sums to 15);
numbers 1–10 are paired with directions and the five phases (1/6 water at
north, 2/7 fire at south, 3/8 wood at east, 4/9 metal at west, 5/10 earth
at center), odd = yang, even = yin. Sources: hrpub.org, "The
Interpretation of Hetu and Luoshu"
(https://www.hrpub.org/download/20200630/LLS4-19314415.pdf); scribd
explainers on the Lo Shu grid.

**Why it fails.** The numbers map to the **cosmos** — directions,
elements, trigrams — never to script units, never to words. This is
number *symbolism*, not a letter-number table. (It is also Chinese in
origin, adopted into Japanese onmyōdō thought, not a Japanese invention.)
NEGATIVE.

### 1d. Kotodama (言霊, "word-spirit") — NEGATIVE (the important negative)

**What it is.** The native Japanese belief that words carry spirit/power —
that uttering a thing can bring it about (kotoage), with taboo and
euphemism as its social machinery. Source: thefreelibrary.com, "The word
'is' the thing: The 'kotodama' belief in Japanese communication"
(https://www.thefreelibrary.com/The+word+%22is%22+the+thing%3A+The+%22kotodama%22+belief+in+Japanese...-a083677628).

**Why it matters that it fails.** Kotodama is the closest Japan comes to a
native *word-magic* tradition — and it is **explicitly non-numeric**.
Power inheres in utterance, not in number. Anyone tempted to claim
Japanese "word numerology" as ancient should first answer kotodama: the
old word-power here never counted anything. NEGATIVE.

### 1e. Gojūon / iroha ordering — NEGATIVE

Japan has letter *orderings* (the gojūon 50-sound table; the iroha poem)
but **no traditional numeric values attached to them**. Numbering kana
1–46 is a modern pedagogical convenience, not a weighing table.
NEGATIVE.

### 1f. Modern 数霊 (kazudama/kazutama) movements — genuine but CONSTRUCTED, not attested

**What exists.** Modern Japanese new-spirituality movements assign numbers
to kana — e.g., the Katakamuna school's 数霊 (kazutama) calculator web app
(https://katakamu-na.com/kazutama.php), and Shinto-kotodama lecturers
treating the Kojiki's "numbers of the universe." These are **late-20th /
21st-century inventions**: web apps, YouTube lectures, seminar circuits.

**Standing.** Real phenomena, wrong tier. They are CONSTRUCTED systems by
modern authors, not an attested native letter-number tradition. Noted so
no one mistakes them for ancient; not used as a table. This is a finding,
not a failure — and it sharpens the audit: when Japan *does* do
kana-numerology, it builds it new, which is itself evidence there was no
old table to inherit.

## 2. THE FALLBACK WEIGHINGS — full chains

Pipeline, stated without elision:

1. **English → katakana**: the scribe's stated choice per standard
   loanword conventions (variants named, never hidden).
2. **Katakana → Hepburn romaji** (トゥ→"tu", ヴ→"vu", ー→macron).
3. **Normalization (CONSTRUCTED, loss recorded)**: macrons stripped to
   base vowels (Japanese vowel *length* is phonemic — this deletes it);
   the nakaguro ・ dropped (romaji-silent).
4. **Romaji → Hebrew** via the repo's `LATIN_TO_HEBREW` with greedy
   digraphs (ch/sh/th) tried first at each position. **No digraph fired
   in any of the 11 terms** — observed, not assumed.
5. **Weigh** in mispar hechrechi (imported repo table, ATTESTED).

### The split tier, stated carefully

- The **TABLE** (Hebrew values א=1…ת=400) is **ATTESTED** — the
  tradition's own numbers, imported not re-declared.
- The **WORD-FORM** (everything before the Hebrew letters: katakana
  choice, romanization, normalization, the repo's constructed map) is
  **CONSTRUCTED** — the scribe's choices at every step.
- CSV tier reads `CONSTRUCTED form -> ATTESTED table`. Per METHOD.md, any
  convergent point here would be a **hypothesis for the red pen, never
  doctrine**. There were none (see §4).

- Vowel length (macrons ī/ē/ā) deleted at normalization — phonemic in

- ヴ→v→ו merges with u→ו at the Hebrew step (distinction kept at kana,

  bridge-table is meant to display).
- Q→K at the katakana step (pre-Hebrew loss).
- No digraph (ch/sh/th) fired anywhere in the corpus.

## 3. THE STROKE-COUNT EXPERIMENT — CONSTRUCTED, full skepticism

**Stated standard.** Katakana stroke counts from standard stroke order
(the counts Japanese stroke-count references and seimei-handan
calculators use for kana): ア2 イ2 ウ3 エ3 オ3 カ2 キ3 ク1 ケ3 コ2 サ3
シ3 ス2 セ2 ソ1 タ3 チ3 ツ3 テ3 ト2 ナ2 ニ2 ヌ2 ネ4 ノ1 ハ2 ヒ2 フ1 ヘ1
ホ4 マ2 ミ3 ム2 メ2 モ2 ヤ3 ユ2 ヨ3 ラ3 リ2 ル3 レ1 ロ3 ワ2 ヲ4 ン1;
dakuten ゛=2 strokes (two small marks), handakuten ゜=1 (one circle),
long vowel ー=1, small kana = large-form count (ゥ=ウ=3). Katakana have
**fixed** stroke counts — which is precisely why they were chosen: it
removes the kanji-selection arbitrariness *within* the experiment, so the
remaining arbitrariness (which script? which reading?) stands out clean.

**What no tradition does.** Seimei handan counts strokes of *personal
names in kanji*. No tradition counts the strokes of loanword katakana to
weigh foreign words. The counts are orthographic fact; the *weighing* is
the scribe's construction. Tier: CONSTRUCTED.

### The arbitrariness demonstration — why kanji selection would be fraud

The experiment is *information-loss measurement*, not a table. Proof:
take CELL. All of these are defensible Japanese writings of "cell":

| Writing | Reading | Strokes | "Value" |
|---|---|---|---|
| セル | seru (loanword) | 2+3 | **5** |
| 細胞 | saibō (biological cell) | 11+9 | **20** |
| 独房 | dokubō (prison cell) | 9+10 | **19** |
| 監房 | kanbō (prison cell) | 15+10 | **25** |
| 部屋 | heya (monastic cell/room) | 11+9 | **20** |

Same word, five writings, four different "values." More:

- **TRVVTH**: トゥルヴヴス = 20 · トゥルース ("truth") = 2+3+3+1+2 = 11 ·
  真実 = 10+8 = 18 · 真理 = 10+11 = 21.
- **AXONEME**: アクソニーム = 9 · 軸糸 (jikushi, the actual Japanese term
  for "axoneme") = 12+6 = 18.
- **LIBERTY**: リバティー = 12 · 自由 = 6+5 = 11.
- **THELEMA**: テレーマ = 7 · 意志 ("will") = 13+7 = 20.

The "value" is a function of the scribe's script choice, not of the
word. Any kanji-based weighing that does not state and defend its
character selection is choosing its own answer. This is why the
experiment uses katakana (fixed counts, stated standard) and still
reports CONSTRUCTED: even the *script* choice is mine.

4. **Stroke standard is stated, not universal.** Dakuten=2/handakuten=1
   follows standard stroke order; some popular counters differ on edge
   cases (ヴ). The experiment's point survives regardless: the method is
   constructed.
